What this is

Why the site exists.

This site is my legacy. Everything I am, in one place, and none of it buried.

My whole life is already on my devices, and it is unreadable. Notes is a pile. Files is a pile. Photos is years deep and sorted by nothing but the day I happened to take the picture. Every single thing in there matters to me, and not one of it is findable.

So I rebuilt it. This is the same material (the apps I have shipped, the school years, the places I have been, my faith, the worlds I grew up inside) taken out of three overcrowded apps and laid out properly: uncrowded, one click from the front door, and made beautiful on purpose rather than by accident.

And it is the one place where the two halves of me are the same thing. I love computer science and I love all of this, and here they are not separate hobbies: every page is hand-written HTML, CSS and JavaScript, which means the technology I love is the material my personality is made out of. That is the part I like best.

WWDC 2024

Cupertino · Apple Park.

Every June, Apple holds its Worldwide Developers Conference (WWDC) at Apple Park in Cupertino, a week where developers from all over the world gather for Apple’s biggest announcements, hands-on labs, and sessions. I had the chance to attend in 2024, and it was unforgettable: walking the Apple Park campus, meeting Tim Cook, and standing under the WWDC24 sign was an absolute pleasure, easily one of the highlights of my life so far. Apple Park itself is genuinely breathtaking in person.

Swift Student Challenge

🏆 Issued by AppleJune 2024Awarded in my senior year of high school

The reason I was there was the Swift Student Challenge, Apple’s annual competition where students around the world build an interactive app in Swift and tell the story behind it. Each year only a few hundred students are selected out of tens of thousands of submissions. I won in 2024 for Al-Quran, my app for learning and studying the Holy Quran, which also earned a personal congratulatory letter from Apple’s Worldwide Developer Relations team. Being recognized among student developers from across the globe, and getting to celebrate it at Apple Park, meant the world to me.

I was selected from thousands of high school and college students worldwide while still in high school, which is the part I am proudest of: the win came in my senior year at Trabuco Hills, and the invitation meant I flew to Cupertino days after graduating, still technically a high schooler. The year before, in December 2023, I had won the Congressional App Challenge for Al-Islam, also as a senior, so both honors on my high school page were earned before I started at UCI.

Every winner is listed publicly on WWDCScholars, the community directory of Swift Student Challenge and WWDC Scholarship winners going back to 2012. The 2024 cohort is ordered alphabetically by first name, so Abubakr is the first name on the page.
